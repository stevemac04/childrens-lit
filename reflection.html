My Reflection

What these three books hold together — and what they ask of readers.

Three mirrors, three windows

Eyes That Speak to the Stars faces a wound — a stereotype drawn on a classmate’s paper — and answers it with family lineage and pride. The Ugly Vegetables starts from embarrassment about a Chinese garden and ends in neighborhood sharing. I Dream of Popo holds love across migration, language shift, and aging.

Together they refuse a single “Chinese American story.” One is about racialized features and boys’ belonging; one about food and everyday difference in the suburbs; one about Taiwan, grandmother love, and distance that never fully closes.

Own Voices and who gets to tell it

All three are #OwnVoices in the racial/ethnic sense: Ho, Lin, and Blackburne write from Chinese or Taiwanese American experience; Kuo illustrates from that same ground for Popo. That matters because the emotional details — Agong, soup that “smells best of all,” dreaming of Popo in English — come from lived texture, not tourism.
