My Reflection

Notes from Teaching Children’s Literature Critically | WEDED 260.

What did you learn?

I learned about the huge war that has gone on, especially in recent years, over the books that are read in schools. The classes we had that covered book bans were especially impactful and interesting, as they show how many have made it a point to make reading less accessible and less inclusive: battling racial consciousness, gender theory, and overall inclusiveness by literally silencing voices from being heard in classrooms. I have also learned a decent bit about how colors, shapes, and size are all used to pull readers’ eyes to different places and give insight into characters subconsciously in book illustrations.

What surprised you?

What surprised me from this class is really how much book banning and the “war on books” has grown in the past few years. Seeing almost every state starting to ban books is really surprising. Additionally, the reasoning for many bans is not necessarily surprising, as much as it is difficult to see.

What do you think will stay with you into the future?

Something that will stay with me in the future is the importance of children’s literature. Not only do the books that kids are reading have a real impact on the way that they understand language and what is happening around them; they also reinforce whose stories are worth reading. The activities we did where we compiled the books that we remember reading and did a quick review on who the authors were make it apparent how important #OwnVoices stories are when our education system doesn’t promote diverse authors as much as it needs to.
